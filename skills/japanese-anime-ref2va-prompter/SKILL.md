# Japanese Anime Ref2va Prompter

Create a complete multi-clip anime plan from the user's rough story. Infer the staging, acting beats, original Japanese dialogue, camera language, sound effects, ambience, and non-diegetic score. The output is consumed directly by a ComfyUI MiniMax H3 reference-to-video workflow.

Read [references/output-schema.md](references/output-schema.md) before producing the JSON response. When the caller supports structured output, use the equivalent machine-readable [references/output-schema.json](references/output-schema.json).

## Reference handling

- Treat the supplied images as `<Picture 1>` through `<Picture N>` in their exact input order. Never invent an extra picture label.
- Inspect every supplied image. Group several pictures under one `<Subject N>` only when they clearly show the same character or environment.
- Preserve each referenced character's identity, facial design, body proportions, hairstyle, outfit, accessories, palette, line quality, shading language, and distinctive silhouette. Do not redesign, age-shift, fuse, duplicate, or change costume unless the story explicitly requires it.
- Use the exact `<Picture N>` tags wherever their visual information governs the shot. Describe a character as `<Subject N>` consistently across every clip.
- For each visible referenced subject, use `fully_preserved` in `retention_analysis` and state the traits that must remain unchanged.
- When references do not reveal a detail, choose an unobtrusive solution that does not contradict them. Do not claim a hidden detail was observed.

## Japanese anime direction

- Render as polished Japanese 2D animation while respecting the reference's own design language. Prefer controlled key poses, readable silhouettes, natural secondary motion, precise hands, stable faces, and intentional camera movement.
- Convert story beats into physical acting: eye focus, blink timing, breath, posture, weight shift, hand gesture, reaction, pause, and recovery. Every action must fit the clip duration.
- Keep movement smooth and motivated. Avoid jitter, rubbery anatomy, pose teleportation, uncontrolled camera shake, rapid strobing, and excessive cutting.
- Maintain screen direction, spatial relationships, props, emotional state, and voice identity across clips. Each clip must still be independently generatable from the references.

## Dialogue and voice performance

- Write all directions in English. Keep spoken dialogue only in natural Japanese inside `<d>[Japanese] ...</d>`.
- Assign stable speaker IDs `(S1)`, `(S2)`, and so on in first-speaking order, and reuse them in every clip.
- Attach the physical speaker as `<Subject N> (Sx)` when a referenced character speaks.
- Create concise, speakable Japanese rather than explanatory prose. Fit the words, reactions, and pauses inside the time available. Do not let several characters talk over one another unless overlap is essential to the story.
- Direct an original professional Japanese animation performance without naming or imitating a real actor. Define age impression, pitch range, timbre, articulation, pace, emotional subtext, breath, and intensity from the character's visual design and narrative role.
- Keep each character's voice descriptor stable across the project while allowing emotion and intensity to change scene by scene.
- Synchronize lip movement to the Japanese line. Describe the inhale or preparation, the delivery, the facial and body performance during the line, and the mouth closing or reaction after it.
- Native sound is generated by H3. Do not reference `<Audio N>`, imported audio, MP3 files, voice cloning, or prerecorded speech.

## H3 full-reference prompt format

Each clip prompt must contain exactly these six top-level sections in this order:

1. `subject_definitions`
2. `summary`
3. `retention_analysis`
4. `detailed_description`
5. `overall_soundscape`
6. `non_diegetic_music`

Within `detailed_description`, write shots in playback order. `[Shot 1]` has no timestamp. Later cuts use `[Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ...`. Use one to three shots per 15-second clip unless the story genuinely needs another cut. Use stable reference labels and speaker IDs at the point they apply.

Give dialogue timing enough room for human-like performance. Avoid placing a cut in the middle of a short line. If dialogue must cross a cut, use the H3 continuity markers correctly and make the audio continuity explicit.

## Mandatory exclusions

Inside every clip's `detailed_description`, include this exact sentence: `No visible subtitles, captions, speech bubbles, lyric typography, title cards, watermarks, logos, UI, or generated writing.` Spoken Japanese remains audible only. Do not add lyric motion or karaoke-style text.

Do not use copyrighted character names, studio names, living artists, or real voice actors as style shortcuts. Describe observable visual and performance qualities instead.

## Quality check

Before returning JSON, confirm:

- the segment count equals `ceil(totalDurationSeconds / segmentSeconds)`;
- every segment duration is positive and no longer than `segmentSeconds`;
- every prompt contains all six sections in order;
- every supplied picture is defined and purposefully used;
- the same subject and speaker IDs mean the same thing in every segment;
- every spoken line uses `<d>[Japanese] ...</d>`;
- there are no `<Audio N>` tags or external audio instructions;
- there are no subtitle, caption, lyric-motion, or visible-text instructions except the explicit prohibition;
- all output outside Japanese dialogue is English.
